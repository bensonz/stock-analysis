## Win-rate methodology (added after the audit; it revises two findings above)

`29.6%` = closed trades with `returnPct > 0`, pooled, unweighted, keyed on exit
date. Three defects, in increasing order of severity:

1. **Pooled across six strategy eras.** Attributed by *entry* date (the ruleset
   that made the pick), the current system is nothing like the average.
2. **Keyed on exit date**, so a trade selected by one ruleset scores against its
   successor.
3. **Censored, and the censoring is biased.** The book holds winners and cuts
   losers *by design*, so open positions skew winner and closed skew loser. The
   07-31 era reads **0%** on closed trades and **60%** once its 6 open positions
   are marked — same era, same trades, opposite verdict.

| era (by entry) | n | closed | marked | fixed-10d |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 03-24 入场护栏 | 29 | 48.3% | 48.3% | 51.7% |
| 07-22 RPS cap removal *(reverted as falsified)* | 10 | 0% | 10.0% | **70.0%** |
| 07-31 RPS band restored | 10 | 0% | 60.0% | 100% (n=4) |
| **whole book** | 54 | **29.6%** | — | **51.9%** |

**Weekly tracking won't work.** Median 2 trades/week; 12 of 19 weeks come out at
exactly 0% or 100%. It cannot separate a strategy change from a coin flip.

**Use fixed-horizon instead** (`scripts/win_rate.py`): mark every entry 10
sessions after entry regardless of when it actually closed. Uncensored, complete,
era-comparable, and available a fixed two weeks after any change.

### What that reveals

Fixed-horizon win rate is **51.9%** vs **29.6%** realized — but the means are
**−0.12%** vs **−0.78%**, a gap of only 0.66pp. Stops convert would-have-recovered
positions into small realized losses while also cutting the disasters: near-neutral
on the mean, devastating to the win rate.

**A low win rate is the signature of tight stops, not evidence of bad selection.**
The spec's "win rate <40% → strategy is broken" heuristic is wrong for this system
and should be replaced with the fixed-horizon number or with expectancy.

**Open question this raises.** The 07-22 RPS-cap cohort was reverted on 07-31 as
falsified, on realized returns of −2.89%. At a fixed 10-day horizon those same
entries returned **+6.86%, 70% win** — second-best of any era. If that holds, the
07-22 entries were fine and the *exits* gave the value back, meaning the revert may
have fixed the wrong end. n=10, so this is a hypothesis, not a finding. Worth a
targeted look before the next strategy change.
